# 05 · Staying legal: cold calling and selling in Australia

> ⚠️ **This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice.** The rules below are how they're generally understood as of late 2026. Before you start, read the official guides linked at the bottom (they're free and short). If anything is unclear, call Consumer Affairs Victoria or ask the ACMA. Fines for breaking these rules are serious.

---

## 1. Phone calls: Telemarketing Industry Standard (ACMA)

These rules apply to telemarketing calls **including calls to businesses**.

**When you can call:**

| Day | Allowed hours |
|---|---|
| Monday – Friday | **9am – 8pm** |
| Saturday | **9am – 5pm** |
| Sunday | ❌ **No calls** |
| National public holidays | ❌ **No calls** |

The hours go by the time where the **person you're calling** is.

**On every call you must:**
- Have your **caller ID turned on** (never call from a private or blocked number).
- Say **your name, your business name and why you're calling** early in the call.
- If asked, tell them how they can contact you or make a complaint.
- **End the call straight away** if they ask you to.
- Not mislead them or pressure them.

## 2. Do Not Call Register

- People, and many businesses, especially sole traders using their mobile, put their number on the **Do Not Call Register**. You **must not** make telemarketing calls to registered numbers unless the person has given you consent.
- **"Wash" your list before calling:** check your numbers against the register at **donotcall.gov.au** (register as a telemarketer or "wash list" user). There's a small free allowance of numbers you can check. Look up the current limit on the site.
- Washing results go out of date, so wash again **every 30 days**.
- Keep your **own do-not-call list** of anyone who asks you to stop, and never call them again.

## 3. Emails and SMS: Spam Act 2003

Every marketing email or text needs all three of these:
1. **Consent.** Either *express* (they said yes, on a call or through your form) or *inferred*. Inferred consent counts when their email or mobile is **clearly published** (on their website, Google listing or Facebook), your message relates to **their business**, and they haven't written "no unsolicited messages".
2. **Identify yourself:** your name, business name and contact details.
3. **Unsubscribe:** a working way to opt out in every message, acted on within **5 business days**.

Don't buy email lists. Don't send bulk blasts. Send one-to-one, relevant emails only.

## 4. Unsolicited selling: Australian Consumer Law ⚠️ Important

The Australian Consumer Law's protections for "consumers" cover **businesses buying services under $100,000**, which includes all your packages. A sale that comes from contact **you started** (a cold call, a cold email or walking into a shop) and is worth **more than $100** can be an **unsolicited consumer agreement**.

**What that means for you:**
- The customer gets a **10 business day cooling-off period**. They can cancel for any reason.
- You must give them a **written copy of the agreement** with the required cooling-off information and a cancellation form.
- During the cooling-off period you **must not take payment** or start the paid work.
- For in-person visits (walk-ins) there are also **set hours**: generally 9am–6pm weekdays and 9am–5pm Saturdays, never Sundays or public holidays. You must **leave immediately** if asked.
- Agreeing to a meeting during a cold call **doesn't** make the later sale "invited". It's still treated as unsolicited.

**How this kit handles it:**
1. The **free mockup** is free, so there's no agreement and no problem.
2. When they say yes, send the agreement (`09-service-agreement-outline.md`) with the cooling-off notice.
3. Take the **deposit and start the paid build only once the 10 business days are up.** Use that time to collect their photos and content.
4. **Leads that come to you** (through your website form, a referral, or them calling you first) aren't unsolicited, so these rules don't apply. That's another reason to build up referrals and Google reviews.

**Before you sign your first client, read the ACCC's "Unsolicited selling" guide and Consumer Affairs Victoria's guidance**, and get the agreement template checked.

## 5. Running the business

- **ABN:** apply for free at **abr.gov.au** before you invoice anyone. Without an ABN, business clients may have to withhold 47% of your payment for the tax office.
- **Business name:** to trade as "Marshall Web Co." rather than your own name, you must register the business name with **ASIC** (about $45 a year) **before** you use it with customers.
- **GST:** not required until your turnover reaches **$75,000 a year**. Until then, invoices say "No GST has been charged" and your prices don't include GST.
- **Tax:** income from the business goes on your personal tax return. **Set aside about 20–30% of profit** for tax. The ATO's "starting a business" pages explain it.
- **Privacy:** your website's privacy policy covers how you handle leads' details. Keep your tracker private.
- **Insurance (later):** once you have regular clients, look at professional indemnity insurance.
- **Under 18?** You can still get an ABN and run a business, but contracts can be trickier. Have a parent or guardian involved and check with Consumer Affairs Victoria.
