# 03 · Objection handling

**The formula:** acknowledge → ask or reframe → offer the free mockup again.
Never argue. If they say no twice, thank them and move on. You have 100 more leads.

---

### "Not interested."
> "Fair enough, I appreciate you being straight with me. Just so I know, is that because you're flat out with work already, or because websites haven't worked for you in the past?"

- **Flat out:** "That's a great problem to have. A website can let you charge more and pick the best jobs. Would it be OK if I sent you the free mockup anyway, no strings, so you've got it for later?"
- **Bad experience:** "That's really common. Most sites are built to look nice, not to make the phone ring. Mine are the opposite. Can I show you the difference with a free mockup?"

### "We already have a website."
> "Great, that puts you ahead of a lot of businesses. I had a look on my phone and noticed **[biggest audit problem]**. Is that something you knew about?"
> "I'm not saying you need a whole new one. I can tell you honestly if a tidy-up would do. Want me to send you a quick free review?"

### "It's too expensive." / "We can't afford it."
> "Totally understand. That's exactly why I have a **$0-upfront option**: from $59 a month, with hosting and changes included. If it brings in even **one extra [job/customer] a month**, it's paid for itself. Does that sound more doable?"

**Founding offer:** "I'm also taking on five founding clients at 40% off right now."

### "Just send me an email."
> "Happy to. So I send you something useful rather than spam, what's the one thing you'd want a website to do for you: more calls, more bookings, or just look more professional?"
> "Perfect. I'll send a **free mockup** to that email in the next few days. Is it OK if I give you a quick call on [day] to see what you think?"

(Getting their permission to send it is good practice, and it means they'll actually open it.)

### "All my work comes from word of mouth."
> "That's the best kind of work, and it means you're good at what you do. Here's the thing though: when someone gets your name from a mate, the first thing they do is **Google you**. If they don't find anything, or they find something that looks old, some of them ring the next name on the list. A website just makes sure word of mouth actually turns into a call."

### "Call me later." / "I'm busy right now."
> "No worries. When's best, **[day] morning or arvo**?"
Book a specific time. "Later" without a time almost always means never.

### "Who are you?" / "How did you get my number?"
> "Sorry, I should have been clearer. I'm [Your name], I run Marshall Web Co. here in Warrnambool. Your number's listed on your **[Google listing / website / Facebook page]**, which is how I found you when I searched for [trade] in [town]."

Always be honest about where you got the number. If they ask you not to call again, apologise and add them to your do-not-call list.

### "I'll do it myself with Wix / Squarespace."
> "You definitely can. Plenty of people do. The catch is usually time: most business owners tell me it takes a few weekends, and then Google Maps and the boring technical stuff gets skipped. I do all of it in a week. Want to see a free mockup so you can compare?"

### "My nephew / mate does websites."
> "Good on him. Is he around to keep it updated when you need changes? If not, I'm local and changes are as easy as texting me. Either way, happy to send a free mockup for comparison."

### "You're new. How do I know you're any good?"
> "That's fair. I'm new, which is exactly why I do the **free mockup first**. You see the work before you spend a cent. And my first five clients get 40% off in exchange for an honest review, so I've got every reason to make it great."

### "Send me your prices."
> "Sure. Packages start at $590 upfront, or $59 a month with nothing upfront. The full list is at **marshallweb.com.au/services**. What I'd suggest is a free mockup first, so you're comparing something real rather than a price list."
